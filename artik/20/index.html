You know, the kind of stuff that makes you question your life choices.

Another hurdle was dealing with the sheer volume of data. We're talking terabytes of logs, which required some clever engineering solutions to handle efficiently. And let’s not forget the ethical considerations; anonymizing user data is crucial. You don't want to inadvertently create a profile of every user's browsing habits!

Ultimately, the results were... promising. Not a groundbreaking revolution, more like a noticeable improvement. The predictive caching reduced average response times by a respectable percentage. But it wasn't the magical 10x speedup I’d initially envisioned. Reality, it seems, is rarely as neat as theory. There's also the question of computational overhead: running a machine learning model adds processing load to the server. This needs careful management to prevent it from becoming a performance bottleneck itself. It's a delicate balancing act.

This journey wasn't just about optimizing Squid; it was about learning the intricate dance between theory and practice.
