How DefiLlama Swap Reduces Slippage in Multi-DEX Trades

**Slippage**—the difference between the expected price of a trade and the executed price—is a major risk in DeFi, especially for large orders or swaps involving illiquid assets. DefiLlama Swap's core strength lies in its ability to mitigate this risk.

It achieves this by executing **multi-DEX trades**, a sophisticated technique where a single swap order is algorithmically broken down into smaller sub-orders. These smaller orders are then routed to different liquidity pools across multiple DEXs.

If a user wants to swap 100,000 USDC for a token, executing the entire amount on a single pool would drastically shift that pool’s price, resulting in high slippage. DefiLlama Swap might instead route 40,000 to Uniswap V3, 30,000 to SushiSwap, and 30,000 to a smaller DEX, all within a single atomic transaction.

By distributing the trade volume across several pools, the price impact on any individual pool is significantly reduced. This results in a better aggregate execution price for the entire trade than a user could achieve manually on any single exchange.

The routing algorithm dynamically compares the expected slippage across potential routes in real-time. It prioritizes paths that offer the deepest liquidity for each segment of the swap, effectively 'skimming' the best price from the top of multiple order books.

The system also enforces a user-defined **slippage tolerance** via its smart contract. If the price moves too unfavorably during the execution time—even across the multi-step route—the entire transaction is automatically reverted, protecting the user's funds.

This intelligent fragmentation ensures that traders, particularly large-volume users, receive a much higher final output amount, making DefiLlama Swap an essential tool for institutional-grade execution on-chain.

Therefore, the aggregator doesn't just find the best price; it ensures that the best price is **actualized** during the live transaction by strategically mitigating the adverse effects of trade size on token price.

In certain complex scenarios, the aggregator might leverage **indirect routes**—for instance, routing a swap through an intermediate token or a specialized stablecoin pool (like Curve) to capture better efficiency before completing the final swap.

The outcome of this algorithmic process is a single, highly optimized **smart contract instruction** that dictates precisely how and where the user's input tokens should be distributed and swapped across the decentralized ecosystem.
