Gas fees fluctuate wildly depending on network congestion. Think of it like rush hour traffic – if you try to swap during peak times (usually evenings and weekends), you'll pay a premium. Try to swap during off-peak hours. I've personally found late nights or early mornings surprisingly cheaper.

Next up: batch your transactions. Instead of making multiple tiny swaps, try combining them into one bigger transaction. It's like buying in bulk at Costco – you save money in the long run. LlamaSwap, if I recall correctly, may even have tools to assist with this.

Then there's the metamask gas fee adjustment. I know, I know – you probably know about this one already. But it bears repeating. Always double-check that "gas fee" slider. You have control! Don't be afraid to slightly increase the "gas limit" (though not too much, you don't want to fail the transaction) if you're worried it might not go through, and remember to keep your "gas price" reasonable. It's a balancing act between speed and cost.

Now, here’s where it gets a little controversial: Layer 2 solutions. This was a topic I wrestled with for a while. They offer lower transaction fees. I was initially skeptical – another layer of complexity? But they do work. This means using networks like Optimism or Arbitrum, which sit on top of Ethereum and provide cheaper alternatives. But it also means navigating a whole new ecosystem, which can take time. It's a cost-benefit analysis – less gas, but more learning curve.

Finally, something I’ve learned the hard way: don't panic-swap. Those impulsive trades when you see a price spike? They're often the most expensive. Take a breath, do some research, plan your moves, and avoid unnecessary urgency. It’ll pay off, literally.
