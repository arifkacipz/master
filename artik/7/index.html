There's also the ever-present concern about performance. HTTPS filtering, especially with DPI, adds overhead. It’s like adding extra weight to your already overloaded sleigh – Santa’s got enough on his plate without slowing down his delivery speed. A sluggish network is a frustrated network, and frustrated users? Well, they tend to find creative ways to circumvent your carefully crafted security measures.

And let's not forget the constant arms race. As soon as you think you've found a solution, the bad guys adapt. It's a bit like trying to win a game of chess against a computer that learns from every move you make. It’s exhausting, yet strangely captivating.

So, where does this leave us? I'm not going to pretend I have all the answers. This article started as a straightforward exploration of the challenges of HTTPS filtering with Squid, and morphed into a rather rambling reflection on the complexities of network security. Maybe that's the point. Network security isn't a clean, linear process. It’s a messy, iterative journey, filled with compromises, late-night debugging sessions, and the constant feeling that you’re always one step behind. But it’s also a journey that, despite its frustrations, is oddly rewarding. Because at the end of the day, knowing that you've played a part in keeping your users safe... well, that makes the fight worthwhile. Even if it feels like herding cats in a blizzard, blindfolded.
